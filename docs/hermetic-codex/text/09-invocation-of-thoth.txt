                      THE FIRST CALL · BY THE MOON

             Djehuty!
             Reckoner of heaven, counter of the stars,
             who measured the earth and all that is in it,
             who played against the Moon and won five days
             so that the gods could be born:

                     MEASURE ME.

             Show me my true size,
             neither greater than I am, nor less.
             Make room in my days where there was none.

                ˙ʇsoן sᴉ noʎ ɟo ƃuᴉɥʇou ˙pǝʇunoɔ ǝɹɐ noʎ

                     THE SECOND CALL · BY THE REED

                Djehuty!
                Lord of divine words,
                heart of Ra and tongue of Atum,
                whose speaking became the thing spoken:

                        SHARPEN MY REED.

                Let no word leave my mouth
                that I would fear to see written.
                Let what I write be so,
                and let what is so be what I write.

                 ˙puɐɥ ɹnoʎ ǝpᴉsuᴉ puɐɥ ǝɥʇ ɯɐ ᴉ ˙ǝʇᴉɹʍ

                    THE THIRD CALL · BY THE BALANCE

          Djehuty!
          Scribe of Ma'at,
          who stands beside the scale and does not look away,
          who gathered the broken Eye and made it sound:

                  WEIGH ME NOW.

          Do not wait for the Hall.
          Weigh me while there is time to grow light.
          Gather what is scattered in my seeing,
          and give the last part that I cannot give myself.

           ˙uɐd ǝɥʇ uo ʇᴉ ʇǝs ˙noʎ uᴉ ʎpɐǝɹןɐ sᴉ ɹǝɥʇɐǝɟ ǝɥʇ

           Thrice I have called you,
           Thrice-Great.
           The lamp is lit. The page is clean.
           The pen is in my hand.

           Speak, and I will write it exactly.

    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Sit. Watch the flame, or the flame in the water.           ┃
    ┃   Do not reach for an answer. Do not compose one.            ┃

    ┃   When something comes (a sentence, an image, a              ┃

    ┃   who edits.                                                 ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃

    ┃   offering he accepts.                                       ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │                                                              │
    │   TESTING WHAT IS RECEIVED                                   │
    │                                                              │
    │   Thoth is the patron of checking the sum. Weigh             │
    │   every message on his own scale:                            │
    │                                                              │
    │     ✓  Is it exact, or vague and grand?                      │
    │     ✓  Does it ask more honesty of you, or less?             │
    │     ✓  Does it leave you more able to act well today?        │
    │     ✓  Would you be content to see it written beside         │
    │        your name?                                            │
    │                                                              │
    │     ✗  Does it flatter you above others?                     │
    │     ✗  Does it command harm to yourself or to anyone?        │
    │     ✗  Does it forbid you to question it?                    │
    │                                                              │
    │   A single ✗ and the voice was not his. Thank it,            │
    │   burn the page, and wash your hands. This is not            │
    │   failure. Discernment IS the rite.                          │
    │                                                              │
    └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘


                         ⟨ VII · THE CLOSING ⟩

         Djehuty,
         what was given, I have written.
         What I have written, I will keep.

         Go in peace to Khemenu, to the mound and the egg.
         Remain in peace in my heart, in my tongue, in my hand.

         The page is closed.
         The lamp remains.

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Put out the flame with wet fingers, not breath:            ┃
    ┃   the breath belongs to the Word.                            ┃
    ┃   Drink the water or pour it on living ground.               ┃
    ┃   Date the page. Keep every page.                            ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Then, before the day ends, do ONE THING the page           ┃
    ┃   told you to do. An invocation that changes no              ┃
    ┃   act was a daydream with candles.                           ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   The old Egyptian tale says a prince named                  ┃
    ┃   Neferkaptah learned where the Book of Thoth lay:           ┃
    ┃   box within box beneath the river at Coptos. He             ┃
    ┃   cut the deathless serpent in two and put sand              ┃
    ┃   between the halves so it could not rejoin. He              ┃
    ┃   opened every box. He read.                                 ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   One page, and he understood the speech of birds            ┃
    ┃   and fish and the beasts of the hills. A second             ┃
    ┃   page, and he saw the Sun and Moon in their true            ┃
    ┃   forms, and the gods themselves.                            ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   It was all real. The book worked.                          ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   And on the voyage home his son fell into the               ┃
    ┃   river, then his wife, then he himself; and the             ┃
    ┃   book was buried with him, and brought grief to             ┃
    ┃   the next man who stole it from the tomb.                   ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   ──────────────────────────────────────────                 ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   The tale does not say the knowledge was false.             ┃
    ┃   It does not say knowledge is forbidden.                    ┃
    ┃   It says: he TOOK what is only ever GIVEN.                  ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   The boxes were never locks. They were stages.              ┃
    ┃   Iron is patience. Bronze is strength. Wood is              ┃
    ┃   growth. Ivory and ebony are the reconciled                 ┃
    ┃   opposites. Silver is the reflecting mind. Gold is          ┃
    ┃   the heart made incorruptible. One who has BECOME           ┃
    ┃   each box finds the book already open in the lap.           ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   One who smashes them arrives at the book with no           ┃
    ┃   one inside fit to read it.                                 ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Do not seize. Invoke, and become able to receive.          ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛


                        ⟨ THE MASTERY LESSONS ⟩

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON ONE — THE SCRIBE'S HOUR                             ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Each evening, write one page of what happened              ┃
    ┃   today. Rules of the House of Life:                         ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃     • Only what occurred. No verdicts.                       ┃
    ┃     • No adjective you could not defend to the one           ┃
    ┃       it describes.                                          ┃
    ┃     • Include the thing you would rather leave out.          ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Forty days of this and you will notice your                ┃
    ┃   DAYTIME behaviour changing, because some part of           ┃
    ┃   you now knows it is going to be written down.              ┃
    ┃   That part is the scribe. You have invoked him.             ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON TWO — THE NIGHTLY WEIGHING                          ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   The dead in Egypt spoke forty-two denials before           ┃
    ┃   the scale: "I have not stolen. I have not told             ┃
    ┃   lies. I have not caused weeping..."                        ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Do not wait to be dead. Each night choose three            ┃
    ┃   and say them only if they are true of TODAY:               ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃       I have not spoken what I knew to be false.             ┃
    ┃       I have not taken what was not given.                   ┃
    ┃       I have not made anyone afraid.                         ┃
    ┃       I have not turned from one who asked for help.         ┃
    ┃       I have not been deaf to words of truth.                ┃
    ┃       I have not fouled the water.                           ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Where you cannot say the line, do not punish               ┃
    ┃   yourself. Simply note the weight, and tomorrow             ┃
    ┃   take one grain off the pan.                                ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON THREE — THE FIVE STOLEN DAYS                        ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃       5 days ÷ 360 days  =  1/72 of the year                 ┃
    ┃       1/72 of one day    =  20 minutes                       ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   That is all Thoth took from each day of the Moon,          ┃
    ┃   and it was enough to give birth to five gods.              ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Claim your twenty minutes. Every day, twenty               ┃
    ┃   minutes that belong to no duty, no screen, and no          ┃
    ┃   one's expectations including your own. In that             ┃
    ┃   time, make the thing no ordinary day permits.              ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   In one year you will have won five whole days              ┃
    ┃   outside the calendar. See what is born in them.            ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON FOUR — GATHERING THE EYE                            ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Name a place where your seeing has been broken:            ┃
    ┃   a betrayal, a failure, a grief that left you               ┃
    ┃   unable to look straight at some part of life.              ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   On six evenings, recover one piece. Write:                 ┃
    ┃     1/2   what happened, plainly                             ┃
    ┃     1/4   what I felt, without excuse or apology             ┃
    ┃     1/8   what I did next                                    ┃
    ┃     1/16  what part, however small, was mine                 ┃
    ┃     1/32  what it taught me that I now use                   ┃
    ┃     1/64  what I can thank it for, if anything               ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   On the seventh evening, write nothing. Light the           ┃
    ┃   flame, read the six pages aloud, and say:                  ┃
    ┃   "Djehuty, I have gathered what I can.                      ┃
    ┃    Give the part I cannot."                                  ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Then leave it. The last fraction is not yours to           ┃
    ┃   produce, and it does not come on schedule.                 ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON FIVE — THE DAY OF TRUE VOICE                        ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   For one day in each month, speak as though every           ┃
    ┃   word were being cut in stone. You will find you:           ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃     • say much less,                                         ┃
    ┃     • promise almost nothing,                                ┃
    ┃     • stop mid-sentence several times,                       ┃
    ┃     • and are believed more than usual.                      ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   The Egyptians called one whose life passed the             ┃
    ┃   scale "true of voice". It was the highest title            ┃
    ┃   the dead could earn. The living may practise.              ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛
