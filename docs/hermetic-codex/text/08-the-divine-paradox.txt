       a dialogue composed in the manner of the Corpus Hermeticum

      TAT      Father, if all is Mind, why should I mend the roof?

      HERMES   Because the rain is also Mind, my son,
               and it will be wet.

      TAT      Then the roof is real?

      HERMES   Real enough to keep off a rain that is real enough
               to soak you. Things are real TO EACH OTHER,
               on their own plane. Ask no more of the word.

      TAT      And above this plane?

      HERMES   Above, there is no one who needs a roof.

      TAT      Shall I go there?

      HERMES   You have never been anywhere else.

               ...

               But mend the roof.

                        ⟨ THE MASTERY LESSONS ⟩

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON ONE — THE DOUBLE GAZE                               ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Choose one object. A stone, a hand, a face.                ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃     1. Look at it as FACT: weight, edge, colour,             ┃
    ┃        history. Grant it full reality.     (3 breaths)       ┃
    ┃     2. Look at it as THOUGHT: an event passing               ┃
    ┃        through the Mind of THE ALL, never the same           ┃
    ┃        twice.                              (3 breaths)       ┃
    ┃     3. Now hold both without choosing.     (3 breaths)       ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   A slight vertigo is correct. That is the ╳.                ┃
    ┃   Daily, until the vertigo becomes spaciousness.             ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON TWO — BOTH HANDS                                    ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Take one trouble you carry today.                          ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LEFT HAND  (Absolute):  "This too is a movement            ┃
    ┃                            in the one Mind. It will          ┃
    ┃                            pass. I am more than it."         ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   RIGHT HAND (Relative):  "And today it is mine to           ┃
    ┃                            meet. What is the next            ┃
    ┃                            honest, practical act?"           ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Then DO the right-hand act with the left-hand              ┃
    ┃   peace. One hand alone is the sleeper or the                ┃
    ┃   half-wise. The Work is done with both.                     ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON THREE — THE HALF-WISE AUDIT                         ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Once each moon, ask without mercy and without              ┃
    ┃   shame:                                                     ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃     • Where did I use a high truth to avoid                  ┃
    ┃       a low duty?                                            ┃
    ┃     • Where did I use a low fear to avoid                    ┃
    ┃       a high truth?                                          ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Name one of each. Repair one of each.                      ┃
    ┃   This audit is what keeps a mystic from becoming            ┃
    ┃   a ruin.                                                    ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON FOUR — THE KOAN OF HERMES                           ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Do not solve this. Carry it.                               ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃         "If the dreamer wakes,                               ┃
    ┃          who is left to say it was a dream?"                 ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   When an answer comes, set it down and keep                 ┃
    ┃   walking. When no answer comes and you are at               ┃
    ┃   peace anyway, you have understood the question.            ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛


    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON FIVE — THE THRESHOLD OF WAKING                      ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Tomorrow, before you move a limb:                          ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃     1. Recall the dream you just left. Admit that            ┃
    ┃        while it lasted, it was simply the world.             ┃
    ┃     2. Feel the bed. Admit that this, now, is simply         ┃
    ┃        the world.                                            ┃
    ┃     3. Ask: "To whom did both appear?"                       ┃
    ┃     4. Do not answer. Rest as the one asked about,           ┃
    ┃        for seven breaths. Then rise and make the bed.        ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Each morning you are handed the whole teaching             ┃
    ┃   free of charge. Most people spend it on the alarm.         ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON SIX — THE ADVERSARY                                 ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Bring to mind one who opposes or has wronged you.          ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   RELATIVE:  "You are not me. Your act has                   ┃
    ┃               consequences. I may refuse it, resist          ┃
    ┃               it, and protect what it threatens."            ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   ABSOLUTE:  "You are me. The one Mind is looking            ┃
    ┃               out of your eyes also, confused in a           ┃
    ┃               way I have been confused."                     ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Now act: unbending toward the deed,                        ┃
    ┃   without hatred toward the doer.                            ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   Dropping the first line makes a doormat.                   ┃
    ┃   Dropping the second makes a tyrant.                        ┃
    ┃   Justice needs both, and so does mercy.                     ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

    ┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   LESSON SEVEN — A DAY ON THE WATERLINE                      ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   For one whole day, mark each hour.                         ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃     ODD HOURS   whisper "dream"  → loosen the grip.          ┃
    ┃                 Whatever you are clutching, hold it          ┃
    ┃                 as a thing already passing.                  ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃     EVEN HOURS  whisper "real"   → tighten the care.         ┃
    ┃                 Whatever you are doing, do it as if          ┃
    ┃                 it could never be undone.                    ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   At night, note which word was harder to obey.              ┃
    ┃   That is the side you lean away from.                       ┃
    ┃   Lean into it for one moon.                                 ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┃   The balanced walker is not one who never tilts,            ┃
    ┃   but one who knows their tilt.                              ┃
    ┃                                                              ┃
    ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛
